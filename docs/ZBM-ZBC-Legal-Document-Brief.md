**Legal Document Brief**

Z Best Media  &  Z Best Clips

*Prepared as a working brief for attorney review — not a final legal document.*

*Purpose of this document: This brief identifies the provisions each Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and Cookie Policy needs to address, based on the founder's own business model and a review of comparable companies' publicly stated terms. It is written to scope attorney review efficiently — it is not drafted or reviewed legal language, and nothing in it should be published or relied upon as-is. An attorney should review, revise, and finalize all language before any of it is used publicly or relied upon contractually.*

# Part One — Z Best Media

Z Best Media operates across four disciplines: Digital Advertising, Revenue Recovery, Out-of-Home, and Z Best Clips (its creator-distribution arm, covered separately in Part Two). Z Best Media's legal documents are comparatively simpler than Z Best Clips' — there is no contractor-payout system to account for — but still need to cover client service terms, data collection through the site's tools, and advertising-related tracking.

## A. Client Terms of Service

- Scope of work language that can flex across four distinct service lines (Digital Advertising, Revenue Recovery, Out-of-Home, and referrals into Z Best Clips) without needing a separate contract template for each — likely a master services agreement structure with service-specific exhibits or statements of work.
- Payment terms: how invoicing works for flat-fee Digital Advertising retainers, phased/results-tied Revenue Recovery pricing, and project-based Out-of-Home placements. These three pricing models are structurally different and the Terms should account for each rather than assuming one universal payment schedule.

- Intellectual property ownership of creative produced during an engagement — who owns ad creative, campaign assets, and any content produced for a client once the engagement ends or is terminated.
- Liability limitations, particularly around Revenue Recovery and Digital Advertising performance claims — the site's own content deliberately avoids guaranteeing specific results, and the Terms should reinforce that consistently (no implied guarantee of revenue recovered or leads generated).

- Cancellation and termination terms, including notice periods and what happens to in-progress Out-of-Home placements or active ad campaigns upon termination.
- Dispute resolution and governing law — see the cross-cutting note on jurisdiction at the end of this document before finalizing.

## B. Privacy Policy

- Data collected directly: contact form submissions, the Revenue Recovery free assessment tool's inputs, and any information submitted through a paid audit engagement.
- Data collected via automated means: advertising and analytics pixels (Google Ads, Meta) used to run and measure Digital Advertising campaigns — both for the business's own marketing and, where applicable, on behalf of clients.

- Third-party data sharing: whether assessment or contact data is shared with any subprocessor (email platform, CRM, analytics tool) and under what terms.
- California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) / California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA) compliance, since Z Best Media operates out of California — this requires disclosure of data categories collected and a 'Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information' mechanism if any data is shared with ad platforms in a way that qualifies as a sale or share under CPRA's broad definition.

- Data retention and deletion — how long assessment and contact data is kept, and how a person can request deletion.

## C. Cookie Policy

- Categories of cookies used: strictly necessary (site function), analytics (traffic measurement), and advertising (Google/Meta pixel retargeting).
- A consent banner mechanism that allows rejection of non-essential cookies, consistent with CCPA/CPRA requirements for California visitors.

- Clear disclosure of any third-party advertising cookies used to retarget site visitors, since Digital Advertising campaigns likely include retargeting as a tactic.

# Part Two — Z Best Clips

Z Best Clips is a two-sided marketplace: independent creators ("clippers") who produce and post short-form content, and brand or entertainment clients who fund campaigns. This structure is materially more complex than Z Best Media's, and mirrors — structurally, not verbatim — the way established platforms in this space (including Whop's Content Rewards product) separate their terms by audience. The research behind this brief also surfaced a real, recurring pattern of creator complaints in this industry around fraud-flagging and appeals — bot-detection disputes near payout thresholds, submissions rejected after views had already accrued, and limited ability to contest a flag. Z Best Clips' terms should be written to avoid generating the same category of dispute, not merely to cover the same topics.

## A. Terms of Service — Clippers

- Independent contractor classification, stated explicitly and unambiguously. This is the single highest legal-risk item in the entire document set — an attorney should confirm the classification language meets current federal and California independent-contractor standards (California in particular applies a stricter test than many states).
- Payout mechanics: how the reward rate (per 1,000 verified views) is calculated, how the minimum payout threshold and maximum payout cap work, and how the optional flat fee bonus is disclosed and paid.

- A fraud-detection process with mandatory human review before any submission is rejected for suspected bot or fraudulent activity — not a fully automated rejection. Building in a human-review requirement from the outset is both fairer to clippers and a direct response to the documented complaint pattern referenced above.
- A clear, time-bound appeal process for any flagged or rejected submission — one appeal per flagged submission, a written explanation requirement, and a stated maximum response time. This is the area most likely to generate disputes if left vague.

- Content licensing terms: what rights the clipper retains in a posted clip, what rights the client/brand receives, and what happens to the content after a campaign ends — this differs materially between Entertainment campaigns (rented distribution, clipper keeps the account and audience) and Brand campaigns (owned distribution, content posts to the client's account).
- Related-account fraud provisions — the ability to evaluate and act on behavior across multiple accounts reasonably believed to be operated by the same person.

- Age requirement (18 and older), with a stated policy for handling and deleting any data discovered to belong to a minor.
- Governing law and dispute resolution clause, including whether arbitration and a class action waiver will be used — see the cross-cutting jurisdiction note at the end of this document.

## B. Terms of Service — Brand & Entertainment Clients

- Campaign funding mechanics: how budget is deposited, held, and drawn down as clips are approved, and what happens to unspent budget if a campaign ends early.
- Explicit exclusion of views generated by bots, scripts, macros, or other automated means from any billable total — stated plainly in the client-facing terms, not merely implied.

- Refund or credit conditions if a campaign underperforms, is canceled, or a dispute arises over delivered results.
- Client representations and warranties regarding the content and assets they provide — that they own or have the right to use any source material submitted for a campaign, with indemnification if that representation is false.

- Content ownership and usage rights after a campaign concludes, particularly for Brand campaigns where content posts to the client's own account.
- Distinction in obligations between the two distribution models (Rented for Entertainment, Owned for Brands) — the Terms should reflect that these are structurally different services, not a single uniform offering.

## C. Privacy Policy

- Data categories collected from clippers: identity and account information, payment and tax information (for 1099 reporting), and social media account connections.
- If clippers connect social accounts (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube) for verification purposes, disclose plainly that the connection is read-only, used only to verify ownership of a post and read analytics, never used to post on the clipper's behalf, and always optional.

- Data categories collected from brand/entertainment clients: business and billing information, campaign source material, and payment details.
- Fraud-detection data: what technical and usage signals are used to evaluate submission authenticity, described at a level of transparency consistent with what has become standard in this industry.

- CCPA/CPRA compliance, consistent with Z Best Media's policy given shared California operations.
- Age policy consistent with the Terms of Service (18 and older; deletion procedure if a minor's data is discovered).

## D. Cookie Policy

- Same categories as Z Best Media (necessary, analytics, advertising), plus disclosure of any tracking used specifically to attribute a clipper's posted content back to a campaign for payout verification.

# Cross-Cutting Notes for Attorney Review

The following items apply across both entities and specifically need attorney judgment rather than founder preference, since they involve legal tradeoffs the business shouldn't decide unassisted.

### 1. Governing law and jurisdiction

Comparable platforms in this space (including Whop and its Content Rewards product) designate Delaware as governing law with mandatory arbitration and a class action waiver. Z Best Media and Z Best Clips are California-based operations. An attorney should advise whether California law offers better-suited consumer and contractor protections for this business, or whether Delaware (or another jurisdiction) is preferable for other reasons — this should not be decided by simply matching a competitor's choice.

### 2. Entity structure and DBA filings

Confirm which legal entity each set of Terms should be issued under, given the existing corporate structure (Silverback Holdings, with Pussycat Alley operating as a DBA under it). Whether Z Best Media and Z Best Clips are separate DBAs, separate entities, or divisions of one entity affects how the Terms should be titled and who the contracting party legally is.

### 3. Independent contractor classification review

Given Z Best Clips pays a potentially large number of individual creators as 1099 contractors, an attorney should specifically confirm the classification approach against current California and federal standards before the platform pays its first clipper. This is the single area where a drafting error carries the most direct regulatory exposure.

### 4. Arbitration and class action waiver

A decision on whether to include a mandatory arbitration clause and class action waiver — standard in this industry, but a real tradeoff between limiting litigation exposure and a user's ability to bring disputes to court. This should be a deliberate choice, not a default copied from a competitor.

### 5. Age and minor-data handling

Both entities should adopt a consistent, clearly stated 18-and-older policy with a defined deletion procedure if a minor's account or data is discovered, consistent with how comparable platforms in this space have structured the same requirement.

# How to Use This Brief

This document is organized so each section — Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and Cookie Policy, for each entity — can be handed to an attorney as a scoped work item. The goal is that the attorney's time goes toward reviewing, refining, and finalizing legally sound language against a known set of business facts, rather than discovering the business model, the payout structure, or the two-sided marketplace mechanics from scratch. None of the language above should be published, relied upon, or presented to a user or client until an attorney has reviewed and finalized it.
